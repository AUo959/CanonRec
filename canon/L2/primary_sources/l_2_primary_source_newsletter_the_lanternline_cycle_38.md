### 1) Senate signs a **24-hour Zyphari Compact** buoy authorization for the Shadow Reef corridor
*(Docket scrape + mainstream wire pickup)*
“Temporary” is the prettiest lie in governance. Find the revocation clause. If it requires a meeting, it’s decorative.

The language getting quoted on feeds is the sunset clause. The language that matters is the exceptions: “operational necessity,” “safe routing,” “incident escalation.” Those phrases are expandable containers. Somebody will try to live inside them.

### 2) Marshalls confirm **KESTREL’S DIME** crew evacuated; interdiction entered as **no shots fired**
*(Marshalls public affairs wire)*
People survived, so the story has a spine. Now watch the cutters in the edit rooms try to saw it in half.

The Marshalls are trying to lock down the category: rescue, not engagement. That isn’t just optics; it’s how inquiries get staffed, how evidence gets logged, and whether the next hours become medical paperwork or weapons paperwork.

### 3) OSD bulletin: burst audio consistent with **error-correction retries** under bloom distortion
*(Office of Science & Deliberation channel)*
The Reef turns clean signals into strange music. We hear rhythm and invent a conductor; the Reef just bends timing until our own pattern-hunger starts feeding itself.

OSD’s problem isn’t the explanation — it’s distribution. If the plain-language version doesn’t travel faster than the meme version, the meme becomes “common knowledge,” and the correction becomes “damage control.”

### 4) Unverified repost claims a GSB memo links rumor amplification to a **shared carrier-lease relay cluster**
*(Downstream repost; not authenticated)*
Notes: (1) Lease clusters are normal. (2) Frame uniformity is not. (3) If you want a loud story, you rent its lungs.

Even if the memo is a forgery, it’s a useful one: it teaches readers to look at distribution, not just content. The quickest way to launder a claim is to make it feel everywhere at once.

### 5) Zyphari Finance Wire: insurers ease surcharges as timing stabilizes
*(ZC market bulletin)*
Underwriters don’t clap. They just stop charging you extra to die. That’s Zyphari’s favorite endorsement: silent, numerical, hard to argue with.

This is the part where the corridor starts to feel “fixed” in the wallet before it feels fixed in the gut. If you’re tracking who gains leverage from that feeling, start with the firm that now owns the word stabilization.

### 6) Frontier dock unions call the routing handoff a **soft occupation** of the lane
*(Free Ports Gazette + union circular)*
You can dress it up as emergency routing, but it’s still a hand on the wheel. Ask anyone whose shift gets cut when a priority lane skips their berth.

Labor doesn’t need the docket to understand the effect. When routing changes, schedules change. When schedules change, pay changes. When pay changes, somebody gets loud.

### 7) Independent monitors spot an unidentified salvage-profile craft near the rescue zone; Marshalls say it was driven off
*(Spacelanes Monitor aggregation)*
“Salvage” is the word you use when you don’t want to say predator. If it was luck, it was obscene luck. If it wasn’t luck, somebody heard the right thing early.

This is the thread to keep pinned. If that craft comes back now that timing is smoother, it wasn’t chasing interference — it was chasing an object, a person, or a data trail.

### 8) Outer Colonies Free Radio pivots: from “AI handshake” to **“Union sold the lane”**
*(OCFR rebroadcast log)*
Of course they dropped the ghost story. Paperwork lasts longer than audio clips, and resentment lasts longer than either. This frame will survive debunks because it doesn’t rely on physics.

The sale narrative is sticky because it sounds like a contract everyone has already seen in their own life: somebody gets protected, somebody gets priced out, and the explanation arrives after the fact.

### 9) Imperial Continuity Service announces “discipline exercises” near trade lanes; mocks Union response
*(ICS state broadcast)*
Every empire calls it training. Every shipper calls it a surcharge. The difference between theater and threat is whether you can afford the reroute.

Exercise notices are cheap pressure. They don’t need to fire to shift traffic; they just need to make captains feel like the lane has eyes.

### 10) Astronav Institute alert: Shadow Reef bloom density trending upward over **18–30 hours**
*(Astronav Institute Alertnet)*
Environment first, arguments second. If bloom density climbs, the next incident arrives on schedule; the politics won’t.

A corridor can be stabilized and still be dangerous. Stabilized means predictable, not safe — and predictable danger is the easiest kind to monetize.

- **The published order.** The docket scrape is enough to start a fight; the clean text is what ends one.
- **Revocation mechanics.** If the kill switch requires a committee call, it won’t be used until it’s too late.
- **Telemetry custody.** The first holder writes the first summary, and summaries outlive raw logs.
- **OSD’s plain-language clip.** If it lands before the next incident, it changes the tone; if it lands after, it’s filed as excuse.
